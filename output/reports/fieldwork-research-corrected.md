# JPMorgan Chase vs. Bank of America vs. Wells Fargo

This comparison uses full-year 2025 results for the three consolidated parent companies. JPMorgan generated the most revenue and net income; Bank of America ranked second and Wells Fargo third on those measures. JPMorgan also reported the highest year-end Standardized common equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio. These results describe financial scale and capital ratios, not which stock or banking service is best for an individual.

## Comparable financial results

Dollar amounts are in billions, rounded. Income-statement figures cover January–December 2025; capital ratios are at December 31, 2025. Revenue uses reported figures rather than JPMorgan's managed revenue or Bank of America's fully taxable-equivalent revenue.

## What distinguishes the businesses

- **JPMorgan:** its disclosed operations combine consumer and community banking, commercial and investment banking, and asset and wealth management. Its investment-banking and markets operations are material parts of the business. [JPMorgan earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/19617/000162828026001902/a4q25erfexhibit991narrative.htm)
- **Bank of America:** its reporting segments include Consumer Banking, Global Wealth & Investment Management, Global Banking, and Global Markets. A comparison focused only on regulatory capital misses these distinct revenue and earnings sources. [Bank of America segment results](https://investor.bankofamerica.com/regulatory-and-other-filings/select-sec-filings/content/0000070858-26-000020/bac12312025ex991.htm)
- **Wells Fargo:** its operations include Consumer Banking and Lending, Commercial Banking, Corporate and Investment Banking, and Wealth and Investment Management. Its 2025 release also identifies removal of the Federal Reserve asset cap as a significant development during the year. [Wells Fargo earnings release](https://www.wellsfargo.com/assets/pdf/about/investor-relations/earnings/fourth-quarter-2025-earnings.pdf)

## Capital: what the original report got wrong

The original report assigned a ranking of 104th and $1.824 billion in Tier 1 capital to “Bank of America.” The cited March 31, 2025 table assigns that row to **Bank of America California**, a different legal entity. Its Bank of America row is ranked second at **$193.808 billion**. These are bank-level absolute capital amounts, not consolidated parent-company CET1 ratios, and they are therefore excluded from the comparison table above. [Original ranking source](https://www.usbanklocations.com/bank-rank/tier-1-core-risk-based-capital.html?d=2025-03-31)

The parent-company ratios above use a common date and Standardized approach. Their ordering alone does not establish overall safety or regulatory headroom: each firm's applicable requirements and risk profile also matter. “Strong capital” and “Basel III compliance” should not be inferred merely from a dollar ranking or the existence of a disclosure.

## Scope and remaining limitations

- This is a comparable **FY2025 baseline**, not a September 2026 market snapshot. Newer interim results and current valuation data have not been incorporated.
- Reported earnings include company-specific charges and other items. The table does not normalize those items or equate larger absolute earnings with higher efficiency.
- A stock comparison needs dated prices, valuation multiples, returns on equity, credit quality, and capital requirements. A consumer-banking comparison instead needs product fees, rates, access, and service features. The original short question does not specify either use case.

The source material establishes differences in results, not contradictory evidence. The original “Competing evidence” section has therefore been removed.
